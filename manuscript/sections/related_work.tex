\section{Related Work}
\paragraph{Perturbation geometry during dense training.}
SAM uses a two-gradient update to optimize loss in a local parameter neighborhood~\cite{sam}. ASAM accounts for parameter rescaling~\cite{asam}, while SSAM restricts perturbations through Fisher-guided or dynamically updated masks~\cite{ssam}. GEAR-SAM uses historical gradient energy to allocate a fixed global perturbation budget across blocks~\cite{gear_sam}. APBR-SAM maintains positive coordinate scales through perturbation-energy feedback and saves this state with the dense model. The resulting geometry guides a structured deletion decision together with activation-based reconstruction.

\paragraph{Preparing dense models for compression.}
CrAM incorporates a compression operator into optimization to improve tolerance to subsequent compression~\cite{cram}. AdaSAP applies importance-dependent perturbations before channel removal and sharpness-aware optimization afterward~\cite{adasap}. These approaches connect robust optimization with compression. APBR-SAM retains dense two-pass optimization and performs support selection after training. The information transferred between stages is a persistent coordinate geometry that expresses deletion relative to the learned scales.

\paragraph{Post-training selection and second-order compensation.}
Magnitude pruning ranks weight sizes~\cite{han_pruning}, and Wanda combines magnitude with input-activation norms~\cite{wanda}. SparseGPT couples support selection with sequential second-order compensation~\cite{sparsegpt}; ROSE modifies its processing order using estimated column and block pruning losses~\cite{rose}. OBC/ExactOBS develops greedy OBS selection with conditional inverse-curvature updates and joint compensation for groups of weights~\cite{obc}. Our procedure uses these reconstruction tools with two distinct selection signals. Geometry-normalized magnitude chooses the first deletion; virtual OBS conditioning supplies the state for choosing its partner. The pair receives one joint update over all currently active coordinates.
