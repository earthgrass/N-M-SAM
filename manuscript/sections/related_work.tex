\section{Related Work}
\subsection{Sharpness-aware and preconditioned optimization}
SAM uses a task-gradient perturbation to seek parameters with low neighborhood loss \cite{sam}. ASAM changes the perturbation geometry to address parameter-scale sensitivity \cite{asam}, while SSAM restricts perturbation support \cite{ssam}. Preconditioned SAM explicitly separates objective and constraint preconditioning \cite{preconditioned}; HC-SAM's normalized direction is an instance of its objective-preconditioning form. GEAR-SAM uses historical gradient-energy statistics to redistribute blockwise perturbations while preserving a global radius \cite{gear}. These precedents rule out claiming that historical statistics, adaptive allocation, or fixed-radius preconditioning are novel in isolation. Our narrower hypothesis concerns the use of \emph{activation inverse-Gram compensability history} to prepare a dense model for an unchanged post-training reconstruction method.

\subsection{Compression-aware training}
CrAM makes training sensitive to compression through compression-aware perturbations \cite{cram}. AdaSAP connects adaptive sharpness-aware perturbations to robust pruning \cite{adasap}. These works already establish that dense optimization and compression need not be designed independently. HC-SAM does not claim to introduce compression-aware training. It instead uses a historical local reconstruction statistic without training a projected sparse network at each mini-batch. Its benefit over an instantaneous reconstruction statistic is an explicit ablation question. Original APBR is retained as a separate internal reference: it uses energy feedback, whereas HC-SAM's state update depends on inverse-Gram estimates and contains no such feedback term.

\subsection{Second-order post-training pruning}
Optimal Brain Compression develops layerwise reconstruction methods including ExactOBS \cite{obc}. SparseGPT shares curvature computations and uses sequential error compensation to make large-scale post-training pruning practical, including semi-structured patterns \cite{sparsegpt}. Wanda uses activation-aware magnitude scores without a compensating weight update \cite{wanda}. ROSE studies feature ordering within SparseGPT-style pruning \cite{rose}. HC-SAM retains the native SparseGPT procedure rather than claiming its selection, ordering, or compensation mathematics as new. Training methods for $N{:}M$ sparsity also directly optimize a structurally constrained model \cite{nm}; our endpoint is instead produced after dense training. The distinction is consequential: HC-SAM incurs additional dense-training calibration, whereas SparseGPT alone can compress an already available model. Any practical argument must account for that extra cost.
