\section{Conclusion and Limitations}
HC-SAM studies whether conditional reconstruction information can connect dense sharpness-aware training with post-training $N{:}M$ compression. It accumulates globally centered inverse-Gram log scales and uses their history to redistribute a fixed perturbation budget, leaving the final native SparseGPT algorithm unchanged. The mathematical properties establish a well-defined signal and allocation mechanism; completed numerical tests establish implementation feasibility. They do not establish better sparse accuracy.

The current study is limited by an unfinished single-seed endpoint, missing Direct-Q controls, unavailable coordinatewise alignment logs, and measurable calibration overhead. Shared input-feature diagonals do not capture the complete interactions of jointly deleted weights. A positive final result would motivate matched multi-seed tests of history and reconstruction alignment; a negative result would constrain this proposed bridge rather than invalidate second-order pruning. Broader architectures, sparsity patterns, and actual sparse-kernel speedups remain outside the present evidence.
